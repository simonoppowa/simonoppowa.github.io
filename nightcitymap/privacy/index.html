If you clear the app data, the app creates a new ID, and the old one is no longer linked to your device.

6. Consent form (Google User Messaging Platform)

On every start, the app asks Google whether a consent form is needed for you. For this, the app sends your IP address, the app ID and device and SDK details; Google works out from the IP address which country you use the app in. In the EU, the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, the app shows the form on first launch and again when Google requires it, for example when the list of ad partners changes. You can change or withdraw your answers at any time in the menu under "Privacy Settings". This item appears only where the form applies.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with Art. 7(1) GDPR: we must be able to prove consent. Storing your answers on the device is strictly necessary for this (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). Outside these countries, we base the check whether consent is needed on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

7. Ads (Google AdMob)

The app is financed by ads from Google AdMob. It shows full-screen ads after a few actions, at most every three minutes, and may show ads when you return to the app, banner ads and ads in the marker details. We switch these formats on and off remotely (section 5).

Switching ads off: while the "Support Development" switch in the menu is off, the app loads and shows no ads and discards ads already loaded. Google's ads SDK is still initialised at start and sends technical details (see "Data") to Google.

Consent: in the consent form (section 6), you decide whether Google and its ad partners may store and read information on your device and use your data for personalised ads, measurement and related purposes. Data: your device's advertising ID (if allowed), the app set ID, IP address, device and app details, an approximate location derived from the IP address, your interactions with ads, and technical diagnostics of the SDK, such as the app's launch time and performance. On newer Android versions, Google may also use Android interfaces for ad measurement. You control the advertising ID in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the menu name may differ by device).

Profiling: with your consent, ads can be tailored to your interests. This does not lead to decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on you (Art. 22 GDPR).

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 25(1) TDDDG). For non-personalised and limited ads, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is financing the free app.

Responsibility and retention: for ads, Google is a separate controller, as is every ad partner you agree to. How long Google keeps data is described in How Google retains data; each partner's retention period is listed in the consent form. More: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

8. Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

To understand how the app is used, we use Google Analytics for Firebase. It records:

  • automatically: first launch, sessions and engagement time, screens viewed, app updates, ads shown and tapped, crashes, device model, operating system, language, a location derived from the IP address (country, region, city), how the app was installed (Google Play install referrer), an app instance ID and, if allowed, the advertising ID;
  • set up by us: the search term when you open a search result, whether the "Support Development" switch is on, and when you change it.

We only analyse the data in aggregate and do not try to identify people. Google Analytics does not store IP addresses.

With consent: in the countries with a consent form, Google Analytics stores identifiers only if you consent in the form. The legal basis is then your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 25(1) TDDDG). Check on a device which choice in the form controls this.

Without consent: before the app knows whether the form applies to you, and if you do not consent, Google Analytics sends events without device or user identifiers, such as app start and screen. Google uses them for modelling. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is improving the app. In all countries without a consent form, the same legal basis applies to all usage statistics.

Google is our processor. Only if data sharing settings and Google signals are off in Analytics. Google deletes event and user data after 2 or 14 months; aggregated reports are kept.

9. Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app crashes or stops responding, it sends a crash report through Firebase Crashlytics (Google). It contains the error trace, the app version, device model, operating system version, free memory and storage, whether the device is rooted, a Crashlytics installation ID and a Firebase installation ID, and the last usage statistics events before the crash (section 8). The app also sends the start of each session with a random session ID, so that Crashlytics can work out how many sessions end without a crash.

In the countries with a consent form, the app sends crash reports and session data only if you agree in the form to the purpose "Store and/or access information on a device". The legal basis is then your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, § 25(1) TDDDG). If you decline, or withdraw under "Privacy Settings", the app sends no more reports and session data from the next app start, and deletes reports still on the device. In all other countries, the app sends crash reports based on Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is a stable app.

Google is our processor and deletes crash reports and identifiers after 90 days.
